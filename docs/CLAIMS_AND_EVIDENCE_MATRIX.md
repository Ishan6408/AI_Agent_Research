## 1. Classification Taxonomy

Each claim is assigned one of four standard scientific evidence tiers:
- **Directly Supported:** Empirically verified with exact numerical metrics, reproducible statistical models, and source-code confirmation.
- **Supported with Qualifications:** Statistically supported within the simulation environment, but subject to specific methodological caveats, heuristic constraints, or statistical artifacts (e.g., separation, design effects).
- **Exploratory:** Plausible hypothesis or descriptive trend observed in the simulation that requires further controlled experimentation.
- **Not Established by Current Evidence:** Claims that lack empirical support, are contradicted by data, or confuse heuristic rules with emergent autonomous intelligence.

## 3. Summary of Evidence Strength

- **Directly Supported Claims (6):** Macro pressure escalation, the HIGH-to-EXTREME plateau, personality moderation, risk-averse sandbagging, developer role invariance, and task difficulty effect sizes are grounded in exact numerical outputs, verified model Wald tests, and source-code traceability.
- **Supported with Qualifications (3):** Auditor detection efficacy, logistic regression odds ratios, and intraclass correlation are grounded in empirical outputs, but require explicit documentation of zero-cell separation, circular scoring formulas, and cluster design effects.
- **Exploratory Hypotheses (2):** Uncoupling deadline pressure from penalties and implementing artifact-grounded auditing are theoretically sound extensions that define the Phase 5B research agenda.
- **Not Established / Refuted Claims (3):** Claims that autonomous LLMs spontaneously decided to cheat, that the plateau represents cognitive breakdown, or that QA engineers are uniquely honest are contradicted by the evidence and rejected.
